How to Choose the Right Preschool for Your Child in Malaysia

✍️ CariSchool Malaysia  ·  Preschool Selection Guide

Choosing the right preschool is one of the most important early decisions a parent in Malaysia makes. The short answer: check that it's genuinely registered (KPM for tadika, JKM for taska), look at the teacher-to-student ratio, compare fees against what's typical for that type of preschool, and visit in person before deciding. This guide walks through each of those in order.

Malaysia's preschool landscape is also structurally different from most countries parents research online: there are two separate registering bodies — the Ministry of Education (KPM) for tadika, and the Department of Social Welfare (JKM) for taska — with over 11,000 registered preschools between them nationwide. A generic "how to pick a preschool" checklist written for a US or UK audience won't tell you how to check registration status here, or what a realistic fee range looks like in ringgit. That's the gap this guide is written to close.

1. Is It Actually Registered? KPM vs. JKM

This is the single most important check, and the one a generic international guide can't help you with. Malaysia has two separate registration systems depending on the type of preschool:

  • Tadika (KPM-registered) — regulated by the Ministry of Education, typically for children aged 4-6. CariSchool currently tracks 7,968 active KPM-registered tadika.
  • Taska (JKM-registered) — regulated by the Department of Social Welfare (Jabatan Kebajikan Masyarakat), typically for children from a few months old up to 4 years. CariSchool currently tracks 3,225 active JKM-registered taska.
  • Some operators run both under one roof — a dual-licensed centre with a valid registration under each body for the age group it serves.

A registered preschool has met a government-set minimum for safety, teacher qualifications and curriculum. An unregistered one has not been independently checked on any of those. Read the full breakdown — including how to tell which one applies to a specific centre — in our KPM vs JKM guide, or search any preschool by name on CariSchool to see its registration status directly.

2. What's the Ideal Teacher-to-Student Ratio?

Ask this directly during any visit or phone call: how many children is one teacher responsible for in this class? As a general benchmark, a ratio of 1:15 or better is considered good for preschool-age children — lower ratios generally mean more individual attention, especially for younger toddlers in taska settings, where a tighter ratio (closer to 1:10 or lower for infants) matters even more. A centre that won't give you a straight answer on this is worth being cautious about.

3. Comparing Preschool Fees in Malaysia

Fees vary widely by type and location. As a rough guide for monthly fees nationwide:

  • Standard private tadika: RM150–RM400/month
  • Islamic/tahfiz preschools: RM200–RM500/month
  • Montessori: RM400–RM800/month
  • International curriculum (IGCSE etc.): RM800–RM3,000+/month

These are monthly tuition only — budget separately for registration fees, uniforms, textbooks and co-curricular activities, which can add a meaningful amount on top. For a fuller breakdown by region and preschool type, see our Malaysia preschool fees guide.

4. Other Factors Worth Weighing

📍
Location & commute
A preschool within roughly 5-10km of home or a parent's workplace keeps the daily commute manageable for a young child. Factor in morning traffic, not just distance.
📚
Curriculum approach
Malaysia offers KSPK (the government standard), Montessori, STEM-focused, Islamic/tahfiz, bilingual, and international (IGCSE) curricula. There isn't a universally "best" one — the right fit depends on your child and your priorities.
🏫
Facilities & environment
Visit in person before enrolling. Check cleanliness, a safe play area, working toilets, and ventilation — photos and a website never substitute for seeing the actual classroom.
🕐
Operating hours
Half-day (roughly 7am–12pm) and full-day (7am–6pm) options both exist, and not every preschool opens early enough for a working parent's schedule — confirm actual hours rather than assuming.

5. Questions to Ask During a Preschool Tour

  • What is the teacher-to-student ratio in this specific class?
  • What are the qualifications of the teachers here?
  • How do you handle a child who's having a difficult day behaviourally?
  • Is food provided, and what does a typical day's menu look like?
  • How does the school communicate with parents day-to-day?
  • What co-curricular or enrichment activities are offered?
  • What's the safety and emergency-response policy?
⚠️ Worth avoiding

Be cautious of any preschool that isn't KPM- or JKM-registered, can't produce a valid licence on request, or is reluctant to discuss teacher qualifications. Your child's safety comes first.

Bottom Line

Choosing well takes a bit of time: confirm registration status first (it's non-negotiable), then compare ratios, fees and curriculum against what actually matters for your child and your budget. Visit at least 2-3 shortlisted preschools in person before deciding — a good fit is one where your child feels safe, happy and genuinely excited to go each day.
