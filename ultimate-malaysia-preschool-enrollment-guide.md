# The Ultimate Malaysia Preschool Enrollment Guide & Checklist

*A practical, no-nonsense guide for Malaysian parents choosing a tadika or taska — updated for 2026.*

---

## Before you start: know which system you're dealing with

This is the single most confusing part for first-time parents, so let's clear it up first.

There are **three different tracks** for early childhood education in Malaysia, and they don't work the same way:

| Track | Who runs it | Cost | How you apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KPM Prasekolah (government)** | Ministry of Education, inside government primary schools (SK, SJKC, SJKT, SABK) | Free | Online via [ePrasekolah](https://eprasekolah.moe.gov.my), open roughly March–May each year for the following academic year, with results announced in early August |
| **Tadika Swasta (private, KPM-registered)** | Private operators — franchises, individual owners, religious bodies | Paid, varies by school | Directly with the school, on the school's own timeline — **not** through ePrasekolah |
| **Taska (childcare, JKM-registered)** | Private operators, for children roughly 0–4 years | Paid, varies by school | Directly with the school |

**The practical takeaway:** if you're browsing a directory like CariSchool for a private tadika or taska, you're in the second or third column — you don't need to wait for the ePrasekolah application window, and you're dealing with the school directly, not a centralised government portal.

---

## KPM vs JKM: what "registered" actually means

Both are real, legally required registrations — but they cover different things:

- **KPM registration** (Kementerian Pendidikan Malaysia) applies to preschools for children roughly 4–6 years old. A KPM-registered private tadika must follow the **KSPK** (Kurikulum Standard Prasekolah Kebangsaan) — Malaysia's national preschool curriculum standard — even though it's privately run.
- **JKM registration** (Jabatan Kebajikan Masyarakat) applies to taska — childcare centres for children roughly 0–4 years old — under the **Child Care Centre Act 1984 (Akta 308)**. Registration certificates are typically valid for **5 years** and must be renewed. As of **1 January 2025**, every registered taska is required to display its registration number visibly on-site — a quick way to sanity-check a centre in person.

**A school can hold either or both**, depending on the age range it serves. If a centre can't show you a valid registration number for the relevant authority, that's a legitimate reason to ask more questions before enrolling.

## The enrollment checklist

Use this when shortlisting and visiting schools:

**Before you visit**
- [ ] Confirm the school's registration status (KPM and/or JKM) and registration number
- [ ] Check the age range the school actually serves against your child's age
- [ ] Note the curriculum approach advertised (see comparison below)
- [ ] Ask about fees upfront: registration fee, monthly fee, and what's included (meals, materials, uniforms)
- [ ] Ask about operating hours and whether they match your work schedule

**During your visit**
- [ ] Ask to see the registration certificate or number displayed on-site
- [ ] Observe the child-to-teacher ratio in an active class
- [ ] Check the physical space: cleanliness, safety features, outdoor/play area
- [ ] Ask how the school handles sick children, allergies, and emergencies
- [ ] Ask what a typical day looks like, hour by hour
- [ ] Ask about the waiting list situation and how far in advance you need to commit

**Before you commit**
- [ ] Get the fee structure in writing (including any deposit or non-refundable registration fee)
- [ ] Confirm what happens if you need to withdraw your child mid-term
- [ ] Ask current parents (if possible) about their experience
- [ ] Check whether the school is listed and verified on a directory like CariSchool, cross-referenced against the official KPM/JKM registry

## KSPK vs Montessori vs Play-Based: which approach fits your child?

Most private tadika in Malaysia blend one of these philosophies with the mandatory KSPK framework. Here's the practical difference:

**KSPK (Kurikulum Standard Prasekolah Kebangsaan)**
The national standard every KPM-registered preschool must follow at minimum, regardless of teaching style. Structured, developmentally staged, covers language (Bahasa Malaysia and often English), numeracy, spiritual/moral education, physical development, and creativity. Think of this as the floor, not a specific teaching style — a school can be "KSPK-compliant" and still run Montessori-style classrooms on top of it.

**Montessori**
Child-led learning with hands-on materials, mixed-age classrooms, and an emphasis on independence and self-paced discovery. Tends to suit children who do well with structured freedom rather than a fixed daily timetable. Often (but not always) a higher fee bracket due to specialised materials and smaller class sizes.

**Play-Based**
Learning through structured play — building, pretend play, sensory activities — with less formal seatwork than a traditional classroom. Popular for the youngest age groups (2–4) where attention spans are short. Good fit for children who need to move and explore to stay engaged.

**In practice:** ask the school directly how much of the day is free play vs. structured activity, and whether they can show you a sample daily schedule — that tells you more than the label on the brochure.

## A note on cost

Fees vary enormously by state, area, and school type — there's no single "average" that means much in practice. Rather than rely on a single number, always get a written fee breakdown per school during your shortlist visits, and compare like-for-like (monthly fee + registration fee + any add-ons like meals or transport).
